California: the maker must sign one and only one box for paragraph 5. The kit repeats the selected paragraph/choice. It does not simulate the signature.
Texas: family and marital-status branches select the court’s exact original. The court ceremony for all four uses the will-maker, two witnesses and a notary together; keep the final notary instructions.
Originals: [Texas Judicial Branch forms](https://www.txcourts.gov/rules-forms/forms/) · [California Courts](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/wills-estates-probate/legal-documents).

Texas also publishes four bilingual English–Spanish editions. All four blank PDFs for the selected edition are requested as a set. The browser chooses the applicable one, fills independently mapped fields, and leaves the three execution fields for the ceremony. For a married person without children, section 2.3 has ten named alternate beneficiary spaces in English and eight in the bilingual publication. A user who has already entered nine or ten names can retain them all in the English edition or explicitly revise the list. Every edition has a pinned hash in config/official-forms.json. California and Maine supply their English originals. WillForFree translates their accompanying documents and identifies the form language in the chat.

Arizona, Colorado, Florida, Kentucky, Minnesota, North Carolina and Ohio receive the same checked-in English blank instrument from the preview link and from every language’s ZIP. The signing guide and introduction follow the selected language. Colorado’s fixed publication expressly addresses substitute descendants under §15-11-603.
