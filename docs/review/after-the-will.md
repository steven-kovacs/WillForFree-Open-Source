# After the will: editorial and source review

Decision, November 16, 2026: build a second guide. The existing guide answers who receives estate property, what an executor does, and how a personal will relates to children and a business. It does not help a visitor work out who could speak to a doctor during their lifetime, how a household bill could be paid, or whether an insurer still has an old beneficiary on file. These are useful questions even if the site never sells a sponsorship.

## What the sources support

- **Care wishes and a decision-maker are distinct.** The [US National Library of Medicine](https://medlineplus.gov/advancedirectives.html) describes both a living will and a health care proxy. The [NHS](https://www.nhs.uk/tests-and-treatments/end-of-life-care/planning-ahead/advance-decision-to-refuse-treatment/) uses “living will” for a decision to refuse particular treatments. [Québec](https://www.quebec.ca/en/health/health-system-and-services/end-of-life-care/advance-medical-directives/description) describes medical directives for particular circumstances; its [protection mandate](https://www.quebec.ca/en/justice-and-civil-status/legal-protection/protection-mandate/about-protection-mandate) serves a different purpose. The guide introduces the term without promising the same instrument or legal effect everywhere.
- **Paying bills during life is a different job from settling an estate.** [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/power-of-attorney) distinguishes financial and health powers, explains their different start conditions, and explicitly limits its instructions to England and Wales. The [US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/consumer-tools/managing-someone-elses-money/) distinguishes an agent managing finances from a trustee. The guide asks what the chosen person would need, without saying incapacity is always required to use financial authority.
- **Beneficiary records deserve a separate check.** [Canada's financial consumer agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/financial-consumer-agency/services/insurance/life.html) explains policy beneficiaries; [Australia's Moneysmart](https://moneysmart.gov.au/how-super-works/who-gets-your-super-if-you-die) explains who a fund can pay, and how nominations can expire. The useful action is to check the provider's records and rules, not to assume either the will or a nomination always wins.
- **Trusts are a question, not everyone's next purchase.** [California Courts](https://selfhelp.courts.ca.gov/wills-estates-probate/legal-documents) and the [CFPB](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/ask-cfpb/what-is-a-revocable-living-trust-en-1775/) explain why transferring the relevant assets matters in a US living trust. [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/trusts-taxes/overview) gives examples of holding property for someone else. Avoid blanket probate, tax or savings claims, and do not transplant a US trust recommendation into another legal system.
- **A record is useful only if the right person can find it.** The [US National Institute on Aging](https://www.nia.nih.gov/health/advance-care-planning/getting-your-affairs-order-checklist-documents-prepare-future) discusses sharing the location of important papers, planning for care and discussing funeral wishes. [New Zealand's public guide](https://www.govt.nz/browse/family-and-whanau/death-and-bereavement/what-to-organise-before-you-die/) also covers advance arrangements and digital accounts. A provider's legacy-contact option can have limits; for example, [Apple](https://support.apple.com/en-ie/102631) excludes stored passwords. The page suggests checking the provider, and asks for no names, paperwork, passwords or wishes.

## Page and commercial decisions

Use “will” as before. Make “After the will…” a separate, directly addressable page connected by ordinary links styled as tabs. The main advice is meaningful in the HTML even without location or JavaScript. [Google Search Central](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/links-crawlable) documents crawlable links; its [helpful content guidance](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/fundamentals/creating-helpful-content) prioritizes a useful answer to the reader's question. This gives the new resource a discoverable address upon release; the research does not establish search volume, ranking lift, sponsor demand or pricing.

The topics overlap the [existing partner examples](../../public/partners/ideas/index.html): legal help with incapacity and trusts, banks and credit unions, pensions and insurance, document storage, executor support and funeral planning. The public cards are independent editorial content. No sponsor offers, referral tracking, lead forms or empty advertising slots. If paid placements are added later, label the paid provider visibly, place it separately from the public reading, and use [Google's sponsored link attribute](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/crawling-indexing/qualify-outbound-links). Do not condition access on contact or pass interview answers to a provider.

## How local is the reading?

The [topic-by-location source register](../../public/after-will-sources.js) records the publisher, destination and any narrower link label. All 97 supported homes have at least one source for that specific country or subdivision: 13 Canadian provinces and territories, 50 US states and DC, four UK nations, eight Australian states and territories, and 21 other countries. A country source can cover a different topic alongside regional reading; a missing topic stays without a link. With no complete location the page gives general actions and points to the existing home picker.

The 97 selections use 132 distinct destinations by default and seven additional French URLs. Regional links include public health services, government publications, courts, legislation, public universities and the Mississippi Access to Justice Commission. A source on one card is not evidence for the other cards. Examples that matter for maintenance:
