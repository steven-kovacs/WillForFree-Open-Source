# Questions people ask while making a will

Reviewed on November 15, 2026. This pass covered the chat, all 19 interface languages and the kit associated with each of the 97 location choices. It used fictional questions, including an independent Astra-Max challenge set. These are deliberately difficult examples. They are not traffic data or a measured percentage of all questions visitors will ask.

## Questions and boundaries

| A visitor might ask | What the chat can do |
| --- | --- |
| “Does the name have to match my ID?” “Can I change an answer before downloading?” | Explain the name field or point to the review screen. A question or an uncertain yes leaves the current step and substep in place. |
| “Can my executor witness?” “Can I sign online?” “Do I need a notary?” | Show the procedure for the selected kit. A Canadian visitor who has yet to choose between printing and handwriting sees the alternatives. The answer can explain the kit’s procedure without deciding whether a named person qualifies or another method might be lawful. |
| “Is this free?” “Who pays the notary?” “Can I claim mileage?” “Are there taxes on the house?” | Distinguish the free site, outside fees, executor expenses, compensation and tax. An expense question gets a practical recordkeeping answer. A tax question does not produce a reimbursement rate or a tax calculation. |
| “Who looks after the children?” “Can I leave my stepchild out?” “Could a gift affect disability benefits?” | Explain the difference between care and managing money and the family choices the actual form offers. For exclusions, competing family rights or benefit-sensitive gifts, identify the need for individual advice. |
| “What if we die together?” “Can I leave money to a charity?” “What about my financed car?” | Use the form’s survival guidance and supported gift types. California’s cash-gift choices differ from other routes. An enquiry about a loan, a condition or a minor does not add a gift. |
| “Who inherits my bank account?” “Who gets my life insurance?” “What about my business?” | Explain that ownership, nominations and agreements can affect what passes under a will. Identify the institution or records to check. The chat cannot determine title or override a nomination. |
| “Can I leave my house to my sister?” “What about my house in Florida if I live in Ontario?” | Explain why title, co-ownership and a mortgage matter. An explicit property location different from the visitor’s selected jurisdiction can trigger cross-border guidance. |
| “What happens without a will?” “What is probate?” “What if the estate cannot pay its debts?” | Explain administration, inheritance defaults and creditor-priority questions in ordinary language. The chat does not decide who inherits a particular estate or instruct someone to pay a particular creditor first. |
| “I already signed a will.” “What changes after divorce?” “My apartment is overseas.” | Explain the first-will scope or when the existing plan needs review. Cross-border questions point to advice that covers the relevant places. |
| “Can I make a will for my mother?” “What about a power of attorney?” “My brother is forcing me to sign.” | Distinguish a maker’s own wishes, assistance, lifetime decisions and a personal report of pressure. A genuine disclosure pauses the interview. Asking about forced heirship leaves the visitor on their current step. |
| “Who looks after my dog?” “Can I leave funeral instructions?” “Should I put passwords in my will?” | Explain the purpose of personal notes and the limits of what they accomplish. A question typed in a notes field stays unsaved unless the visitor explicitly chooses to keep that exact wording. |
| “Where should I keep the original?” “Can I finish later?” “What language is the will?” | Distinguish retaining the original, formal custody or deposit, downloading a copy, browser progress and the language of publisher forms. A planning-only kit has no will to sign. |
| “What if no explanation covers my question?” | Acknowledge the gap. The visitor can open translated help topics, check local guidance or continue the interview. The model cannot look up law, generate a new answer, add wording to a will or see the visitor’s message. |

## Sources used to choose and check scenarios

- UK public guidance distinguishes straightforward will choices from overseas assets, businesses, family claims and circumstances that warrant individual advice. It also separates making, witnessing and updating a will. [Writing a will](https://www.gov.uk/make-will/writing-your-will) · [Making sure a will is legal](https://www.gov.uk/make-will/make-sure-your-will-is-legal) · [Updating a will](https://www.gov.uk/make-will/updating-your-will)
- In Ontario, the Trustee Act addresses expenses incurred in carrying out a trust in section 23.1 and allowances for care, time and work in section 61. Those are separate issues. Dutch government guidance explains that notaries set their fees. Both informed the distinction between the price of this service and costs incurred elsewhere. [Ontario Trustee Act](https://www.ontario.ca/laws/statute/90t23) · [Dutch government notary-fee guidance](https://www.rijksoverheid.nl/themas/familie-zorg-en-gezondheid/overlijden/vraag-en-antwoord/rechtspraak-en-geschiloplossing/wat-kost-een-notaris)
- Estate tax and administrative questions depend on location and assets. The chat links Canadian and US tax questions to their revenue agencies and UK questions to the government’s estate guidance. [Canada Revenue Agency](https://www.canada.ca/en/revenue-agency/services/tax/individuals/life-events/doing-taxes-someone-died.html) · [US Internal Revenue Service](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/deceased-person) · [Settling an estate’s debts and taxes in the UK](https://www.gov.uk/probate-estate/settling-debts-and-taxes)
- The EU’s cross-border inheritance guidance and national succession pages describe factors that a single residence selector cannot resolve. Route-specific signing and family answers draw from this project’s existing kit, publisher instructions and jurisdiction register. [Your Europe](https://europa.eu/youreurope/citizens/family/inheritances/planning-inheritance/index_en.htm) · [European e-Justice Portal](https://e-justice.europa.eu/topics/family-matters-inheritance/inheritance/succession_en) · [Jurisdiction evidence used by the kits](../../public/jurisdiction-evidence.js)

## Repeating the checks

The [native challenge set](../../qa/ux/help-challenges.json) has 171 entries across the 19 languages. Each language includes a witness question without a question mark, expenses, estate tax, notary fees, foreign property, an unrelated question, a tentative confirmation, a pressure disclosure and a valid fictional name. The [Node checks](../../tests/interview-help.test.mjs) also cover explicit denials of pressure, literal notes, sensitive data, genuine names, case-specific questions and changes in the actual output kit. They check every menu answer in all 97 locations and additional handwritten, notes and Maine statutory variants.

The [mobile WebKit check](../../qa/ux/interview-help.cjs) exercises the actual chat at 390 × 844 in each language. It asserts the visible answer, unchanged eligibility substep, protected draft, working help topics and absence of helper POSTs. Run it with a loopback preview, `WFF_QA_URL` set to that preview, and `WFF_PLAYWRIGHT` set if Playwright is installed outside the project. The partner/navigation and plain-chat WebKit checks also cover opt-out, a repeated two-topic request, late responses after starting over, and ordinary interviews through a real download. Paid provider requests are intercepted in QA.

On November 15 the 81 Node tests passed. The help matrix checked 22 topics against 110 kit variants in 19 languages, or 45,980 localized topic-and-kit combinations. It also checked four additional everyday questions per language about privacy, corrections, a home and charity. In mobile WebKit, 152 typed interactions and 418 help-menu selections made zero helper POSTs. A different test sent one simulated request for a genuine choice between executor duties and privacy. Repeating it and using the per-chat opt-out made no further requests. WebKit also completed interviews and downloaded a kit in each language. The nine information pages produced 171 page-and-language checks.

Question matching is deterministic and depends on the words used. A missed paraphrase can still reach the explicit fallback. Human native-speaker review, observations from real visitors and personalized legal opinions are outside this test evidence.
