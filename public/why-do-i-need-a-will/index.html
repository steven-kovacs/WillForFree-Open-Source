Why do I need a will?

Maybe you want your sister to keep your old camera, or a friend to have some of your savings. A will gives you a place to say who should receive those things after you die.

My personal and family plans

What happens if I leave no will?

Local inheritance rules decide who receives the money and property in your estate. Someone important to you, like a close friend, could receive nothing under those rules. Some places also protect a spouse’s or child’s share even when you do make a will.

Who will take care of the paperwork?

In many places, you can name someone you trust to settle bills and carry out your wishes. You may hear this person called an executor. Ask whether they’re willing to do it, and tell them where to find the signed original.

What if my children are still young?

Think about two jobs. Who would you want to care for your children if they needed a guardian? Who would you trust with money you leave them? A will can record your choices where local law allows. A surviving parent’s rights and a court’s decision can affect who cares for a child.

I have a business too

Can I still make my personal will?

Yes, if the form fits what you want. When the chat asks about ownership, choose I own a business. You can continue if you’ve checked what your estate would receive and you want it to go to the same people, in the same shares, as everything else left over. The kit includes a business checklist alongside your will and local signing instructions.

Who owns the things I use for work?

List what you own personally and what belongs to a company, a co-owner or someone else. For example, you may own shares in the company that owns your work van. Your personal will can give away only the rights that pass through your estate.

What have my co-owners and I already agreed to?

Check your business agreements, company rules, licences and any loans secured on the business. Can the person or trustee you chose hold your interest? Must someone buy it when you die? If a buyout applies, your estate may receive money instead of the business interest itself.

What can I do now?

Write down what you own personally, what the business owns and any deals you’ve made with co-owners. Pick one practical question for a local estates professional, such as “If I died this week, who could run payroll, and who could inherit my shares?”

If you want your daughter to inherit the business and your spouse to get everything else, leaving out the business name won’t do that. If you already have a business will, signing another one could cancel it. Check how the documents fit together before you sign. If the chat pauses, choose Download my answers (PDF) to take your choices and agreements to an adviser.

Some locations use a blank form you fill in yourself. There’s a business checklist in those kits too. Read each gift in the form to see what it covers before you sign.
