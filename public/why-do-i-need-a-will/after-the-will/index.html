After the will…

A will says who should receive your property after you die. But who could speak for you in hospital, pay the bills if you were ill, or find the papers your family needs?

These are things to consider, not extra forms you need to finish your will. Pick the one that matters to you.

While you’re alive

Health care and living wills

If you couldn’t speak for yourself, what would you want your care team to know? In some places you can record treatment wishes in an advance directive, sometimes called a living will. Naming someone to speak for you may take a separate step.

One first step

Talk with someone you trust about what matters to you, then ask your care team how to record it.

    Who could pay the bills?

    Naming someone in your will doesn’t let them use your bank account while you’re alive. Ask about a financial power of attorney or its local equivalent if you want someone to be able to manage money for you.

    One first step

    Ask your bank what it would need to let your chosen person act.

      What you leave behind

      Check your beneficiaries

      Life insurance, pensions and some accounts can have their own beneficiary rules. Updating your will may not change the name a provider has on file.

      One first step

      Ask one provider who is on record, how to update the name and whether the nomination expires.

        When a trust might help

        If you want someone to manage an inheritance for a child or another person, ask a local estates professional what arrangements are available. A trust is one option in some places.

        In the United States, a revocable living trust can hold property during your lifetime. Ask which assets would need to be transferred into it for the plan to work.

        One first step

        Ask who would manage the property, for how long and at what cost.

          What your family can find

          Leave a map of your paperwork

          List where to find your signed will, insurance policies and important digital files. Keep passwords and recovery codes out of your will and off this list.

          One first step

          Tell one trusted person where the list is. For online accounts, check whether the provider offers a legacy contact.

            Share your funeral wishes

            The person arranging a funeral may need to act before anyone reads the will. Share any wishes about a service, burial or cremation with them now.

            One first step

            Tell them where to find any arrangements you’ve already made. If organ donation matters to you, check how your local system records your choice.
