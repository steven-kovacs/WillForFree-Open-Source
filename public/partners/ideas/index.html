Where does your business fit?

Start with a question someone asks when making a will. Each example connects that question with a service a business or community organization can offer.

Who depends on my income?

Insurers can offer parents a review of life, disability or critical illness coverage. Credit unions can help families explore education savings, including a Registered Education Savings Plan (RESP) in Canada.

Can my family keep our home?

Mortgage lenders and brokers can offer a rate comparison or a mortgage protection review. A debt counsellor can help a household work out what it can afford on one income.

Do my savings still fit my plans?

Investment firms can review mutual funds, retirement income and the beneficiaries on existing accounts. An adviser can help a client compare options such as annuities or, in Canada, segregated funds.

Who can help if my wishes are complicated?

Law firms can help blended families, people supporting a dependant for life, and families with property in two countries. They can also advise on powers of attorney. A trust company can explain professional executor services, and an accountant can help a family plan for estate taxes.

Couples joining households may have questions about a marriage or cohabitation agreement. Other families want to know how to set up and manage a trust.

What happens to the business or family cottage?

A succession adviser can help co-owners plan a buyout. An insurance broker can explain key person coverage. An appraiser can help relatives value a cottage before they discuss keeping or selling it.

Where can my family find what they need?

Digital vault providers can offer an inventory for accounts and important documents. Executor support services can share a first-steps checklist. Funeral planners, pet care services and family archivists can help people record personal wishes.

A planning service can help families review their arrangements and keep documents and beneficiaries up to date. A digital legacy service can help someone leave letters, recorded stories or a family photo archive.

What can I offer the people I already serve?

Employers can add a free kit to a benefits guide. Member associations can share it at a workshop. Charities can give supporters the link and offer a separate conversation to anyone who asks about legacy giving.

A charitable giving adviser can explain donor-advised funds to supporters who want to give to several causes.
